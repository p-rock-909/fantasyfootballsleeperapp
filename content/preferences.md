# My Draft Preferences

This file is read on every recommendation and treated as the highest authority. It is customized for the 2026 draft board and sleeper/rookie addendum supplied for this league.

## League format and scoring
- **League:** 14-team redraft.
- **Lineup:** 1 QB, 2 RB, 3 WR, 1 TE, 1 WR/TE flex, 1 WR/TE/QB flex, K, DST, 7 bench.
- **Scoring:** **Full PPR**; 1 point per 20 passing yards.
- **Implication:** This is a deep, skill-position-scarcity league. You can start at least five WR/RB players every week before the two flex spots, and full PPR further rewards target volume, routes, receiving RB roles, and high-catch-rate slot/possession WRs.
- **Draft objective:** Maximize weekly scoring and championship equity. Prefer players with credible elite outcomes driven by projected touches/targets, route participation, receiving work, red-zone usage, and offense/QB context.

## Strategy archetype
- **Overall approach:** Adaptive WR-forward Hero-RB / modified Zero-RB. In full PPR with 3 WR plus two flexes, build an early foundation of elite target earners while securing one strong RB with a receiving role or a true bell-cow workload. Do not force a strict WR-WR start; take the best player in the top tier.
- **Core rule:** Best-player-available within tiers through Round 6, but break close RB/WR ties toward WR and pass-catching RBs. Do not pay early for a replaceable 1-QB starter.
- **Rounds 1-3 priorities:**
  - Draft an elite RB or target-dominant WR in Round 1. Elite PPR WRs and receiving backs receive a premium.
  - Aim to leave Round 3 with at least 2 WR and 1 RB, or 2 RB and 1 WR only if both RBs have premium volume/receiving profiles and the WR board is still strong.
  - Prefer a 2-WR / 1-RB opening in close calls.
  - Take Brock Bowers or Trey McBride only if the value is materially below room cost and the RB/WR tier is flat. Do not take QB in the first three rounds.
- **Rounds 4-8 priorities:**
  - Build PPR starting depth: target 3-4 WR and 2-3 RB by the end of Round 8, depending on board value.
  - Prioritize high-route, high-target WRs and RBs who catch passes over touchdown-dependent early-down RBs.
  - Use Round 4-6 as the preferred TE window only for a difference-maker at a value: Colston Loveland first; then Tyler Warren or Tucker Kraft if they fall and the RB/WR alternatives are weaker.
  - Wait on QB unless Josh Allen or Lamar Jackson fall at least 1-2 rounds below expected cost; otherwise target the QB tier in Rounds 7-10.
- **Rounds 9+:** Prioritize contingent RBs with three-down/pass-catching paths, rookie WRs with immediate routes, late TE values, and one discounted QB. Draft K and DST in the final two selections only.

## Positional rules
- **QB:** Wait in this 1-QB league. Full PPR changes WR/RB values, not the structural depth of QB. Default target range is Rounds 7-10; do not be the first manager to trigger a QB run.
  - Preferred values: Justin Herbert, Dak Prescott, Trevor Lawrence, Brock Purdy, Bo Nix, Kyler Murray, Jordan Love, Baker Mayfield, Tyler Shough, C.J. Stroud, Daniel Jones.
  - Elite-QB exception: Josh Allen or Lamar Jackson only if they slide at least 1-2 rounds past room cost and the remaining RB/WR values are clearly inferior.
  - Draft one QB by default. Add a second only if the starter is a volatile late bet or the second is an exceptional late value.
- **RB:** Emphasize receptions, routes, two-minute work, and goal-line access. Have 2 RBs by Round 5 if feasible; target 4-6 RBs total, with later picks concentrated in contingent backs who could inherit passing-down or lead-back workloads.
- **WR:** This is the primary scoring-depth position. Have at least 3 WRs by Round 6-7 and aim for 5 WRs by Round 9-10 when value permits. Prefer target share, route participation, and short/intermediate volume over pure deep-threat profiles.
- **TE:** Pay only for a genuine PPR positional edge. Target Bowers/McBride only at a fall; otherwise Loveland in Round 4, Warren/Kraft in Rounds 5-6, or wait for target-friendly late TE values.
  - Late TE plan: Isaiah Likely, Dalton Kincaid, Dallas Goedert, Mark Andrews, Juwan Johnson, Terrance Ferguson, Brenton Strange, Chigoziem Okonkwo, Kenyon Sadiq.
  - If TE is delayed beyond Round 9, take two complementary late options rather than forcing a mediocre mid-round TE.
- **K / DST:** Last two rounds only, no exceptions unless an unusual room run makes one essential before the final turn.
  - K preference: Brandon Aubrey, Cameron Dicker, Ka'imi Fairbairn, Will Reichard, Harrison Butker, Jason Myers.
  - DST preference: Houston Texans, Seattle Seahawks, Denver Broncos, Los Angeles Rams, New England Patriots, Philadelphia Eagles, Pittsburgh Steelers, Green Bay Packers.
- **Max players from one NFL team:** No hard cap. Allow 2-3 players from a strong offense when each is individually a value. Avoid 3+ players from weak or offense-uncertain teams, particularly when their outcomes depend on the same fragile QB/offensive environment.
- **Handcuffs:** Prefer high-leverage handcuffs to other managers’ RBs over mediocre backups to my own RBs. Handcuff my own RB1 only if the backup is a clear role successor and falls into the appropriate late-round tier.

## Risk tolerance
- **Risk profile:** Medium-high with PPR role discipline. Chase elite volume and contingent league-winning outcomes after core starters are set, but do not draft several early players carrying active injury/availability uncertainty.
- **Injury history tolerance:** Medium. Accept a discounted elite player with a confirmed Week 1 role, but require a meaningful discount for active soft-tissue, knee, foot, ankle, suspension, or role uncertainty.
- **Age:** Be cautious at price with RBs age 29+ and WRs age 31+, but do not auto-exclude a proven player with strong routes and target volume if the price compensates.
- **Rookies:** Favor rookie RBs with an early workload and receiving path, and rookie WRs with immediate routes/target potential. Avoid rookie TEs unless the price is endgame and the role is unusually clear.
- **Floor vs. ceiling:** Early rounds: target reliability through elite volume and stable roles. Middle/late rounds: favor ceiling, route expansion, and contingent workload over low-ceiling veteran bench depth.
- **PPR tiebreaker:** When players have similar workload projections, select the player more likely to run routes and earn targets. Receiving volume is more predictable and valuable than betting on a small touchdown edge.

## Stacking and correlation
- **QB + WR/TE stacks:** Seek natural stacks only when the individual player is already the best value in the tier. A stack is a tiebreaker, not a reason to reach.
- **PPR-friendly stack examples from the supplied board:** Burrow + Chase/Higgins; Lamar + Flowers; Herbert + McConkey/Johnston/Harris; Purdy + Nacua/Adams/Stribling only if player-team assignments remain accurate in the draft platform; Mayfield + Godwin/Irving; Lawrence + Brian Thomas/Parker Washington; Shough + a Saints pass catcher.
- **Same-bye stacking:** Avoid drafting 3 or more essential starters with the same bye when alternatives are equal. Two is acceptable; do not sacrifice player value simply to avoid a two-player bye overlap.
- **Backfield correlation:** Do not roster a starter and handcuff by default. Do so only if the backup would inherit a valuable PPR-friendly role and the roster has enough weekly starter depth.

## Roster construction checkpoints
- **After Round 3:** Prefer at least 2 WR and 1 RB. The acceptable alternative is 2 elite/receiving RB plus 1 WR if WR values clearly remain stronger in the next tier. No early QB.
- **After Round 6:** Prefer 2-3 RB, 3-4 WR, and either one value TE or a clear plan to wait. Do not have both an expensive QB and TE unless both fell substantially below cost.
- **After Round 9:** Prefer 3-4 RB, 4-5 WR, one QB, and one TE. If QB or TE is delayed, make a deliberate paired late-round plan.
- **Final roster:** Maintain enough reliable PPR routes/touches for weekly lineup stability, then use several bench spots for contingent RBs and route-based rookie WRs. Do not fill the bench with replacement-level veterans.
