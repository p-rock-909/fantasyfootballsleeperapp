# My Draft Preferences — Updated for Half-PPR

This file is read on every recommendation and treated as the highest authority. It is customized for the 2026 draft board and sleeper/rookie addendum supplied for this league.

## League format and scoring

- **League:** 14-team redraft.
- **Lineup:** 1 QB, 2 RB, 3 WR, 1 TE, 1 WR/TE flex, 1 WR/TE/QB flex, K, DST, 7 bench.
- **Scoring:** **Half-PPR**; 0.5 points per reception; 1 point per 20 passing yards; 4 points per passing TD; 2 points per two-point conversion; 1 point per 10 rushing/receiving yards; 6 points per rushing/receiving TD; 1 point per PAT; 0.1 points per field-goal yard.
- **Implication:** This is a deep, skill-position-scarcity league. You can start at least five WR/RB players every week before the two flex spots. Targets, routes, receiving-RB roles, and high-catch-rate WRs remain very valuable, but half-PPR increases the relative importance of rushing workload, goal-line access, yardage, touchdown equity, and efficient downfield roles compared with full PPR.
- **Draft objective:** Maximize weekly scoring and championship equity. Prefer players with credible elite outcomes driven by projected touches/targets, snap and route participation, receiving work, red-zone usage, and offense/QB context.

## Strategy archetype

- **Overall approach:** Adaptive Hero-RB / balanced RB-WR. Favor elite target earners because the lineup starts three WRs plus two flexes, but do not force a modified Zero-RB approach. In half-PPR and a 14-team league, prioritize securing two credible RB workloads by the end of Round 5 or 6.
- **Core rule:** Best player available within tiers through Round 6. In close choices, evaluate projected touches/targets and snap share first; then goal-line/red-zone role; then receiving routes/target share; then offense/QB environment; then efficiency. Do not pay early for a replaceable 1-QB starter.
- **Half-PPR tiebreaker:** Receiving volume remains valuable, but do not choose a low-ceiling reception specialist over a reliable 15–20-touch RB with short-yardage work merely because of catch totals.

### Rounds 1–3 priorities

- Draft an elite RB or target-dominant WR in Round 1.
- Aim to leave Round 3 with at least 2 WR and 1 RB, or 2 RB and 1 WR only if both RBs have premium volume, goal-line, or receiving profiles and the WR board remains strong.
- Prefer a 2-WR / 1-RB opening in close calls.
- Take a second premium RB over a similarly ranked WR when the RB has clear workload, touchdown equity, and a better offense/QB environment.
- Take Brock Bowers or Trey McBride only if the value is materially below room cost and the RB/WR tier is flat.
- Do not take QB in the first three rounds.

### Rounds 4–8 priorities

- Build starting depth: target 3–4 WR and 2–3 RB by the end of Round 8, with **three RBs preferred** unless two already have secure, high-end workloads.
- Prioritize high-route/high-target WRs and RBs with complete volume profiles. Give a half-PPR boost to RBs with 15+ projected touches, goal-line access, or meaningful receiving work.
- Secure the second viable RB by Round 5 whenever possible. Round 6 is acceptable only if elite WR value drives the board and viable RB volume remains.
- Use Round 4–6 as the preferred TE window only for a difference-maker at a value: Colston Loveland first; then Tyler Warren or Tucker Kraft if they fall, health is acceptable, and RB/WR alternatives are weaker.
- Wait on QB unless Josh Allen or Lamar Jackson falls at least 1–2 rounds below expected cost. Otherwise target the QB tier in Rounds 7–10.

### Rounds 9+

- Prioritize contingent RBs with three-down, goal-line, or pass-catching inheritance paths.
- Target rookie WRs with immediate routes, realistic target volume, and/or a red-zone pathway.
- Take a late TE value and one discounted QB only when the weekly starting position remains unresolved.
- Draft K and DST in the final two selections only.

## Positional rules

### QB

- Wait in this 1-QB league. Half-PPR changes RB/WR values, not QB’s structural depth.
- Default target range is Rounds 7–10. Do not be the first manager to trigger a QB run.
- Preferred values: Justin Herbert, Dak Prescott, Trevor Lawrence, Brock Purdy, Bo Nix, Kyler Murray, Jordan Love, Baker Mayfield, Tyler Shough, C.J. Stroud, Daniel Jones.
- Elite-QB exception: Josh Allen or Lamar Jackson only if they slide at least 1–2 rounds past room cost and remaining RB/WR values are clearly inferior.
- The 1 point per 20 passing-yards rate moderately boosts falling high-volume passers, but does not justify reaching.
- Draft one QB by default. Add a second only if the starter is a volatile late bet or the second is an exceptional late value.
- Treat the WR/TE/QB flex as a best-score flex, not an instruction to draft a second QB. In most weeks it should be a WR or RB.

### RB

- Prioritize projected touches and snap share first, then goal-line access, then receiving routes/two-minute work.
- Receiving remains a major half-PPR differentiator, but a stable 15–20-touch RB with carries inside the 10 can be more valuable than a 10–12-touch receiving specialist.
- Have 2 RBs by Round 5 if feasible; by Round 6 is acceptable only in a strong WR-driven build.
- Target 5–7 RBs total. Later picks should concentrate on high-leverage contingent backs who could inherit lead-back, passing-down, or goal-line workloads.

### WR

- This is the primary scoring-depth position because three WRs plus two flexes can be started weekly.
- Have at least 3 WRs by Round 6–7 and aim for 4–5 strong WRs by Round 9–10 when value permits.
- Prefer target share and route participation first; then red-zone targets; then air-yard/downfield role; then QB quality and efficiency.
- Favor short/intermediate target volume for weekly stability, but do not automatically fade deep threats. In half-PPR, stable-route WRs with high air-yard shares and red-zone usage can beat low-ceiling possession receivers.

### TE

- Pay only for a genuine half-PPR positional edge: elite route participation, team target share, and red-zone involvement.
- Target Bowers/McBride only at a fall.
- Otherwise target Loveland in Round 4 at value; Warren/Kraft in Rounds 5–6 at a discount; or wait for target-friendly late TE values.
- Late TE plan: Isaiah Likely, Dalton Kincaid, Dallas Goedert, Mark Andrews, Juwan Johnson, Terrance Ferguson, Brenton Strange, Chigoziem Okonkwo, Kenyon Sadiq.
- If TE is delayed beyond Round 9, take two complementary late options rather than forcing a mediocre mid-round TE.

### K / DST

- Draft in the final two rounds only, no exceptions unless an unusual room run makes one essential before the final turn.
- K preference: Brandon Aubrey, Cameron Dicker, Ka'imi Fairbairn, Will Reichard, Harrison Butker, Jason Myers.
- DST preference: Houston Texans, Seattle Seahawks, Denver Broncos, Los Angeles Rams, New England Patriots, Philadelphia Eagles, Pittsburgh Steelers, Green Bay Packers.
- Because each FG yard is worth 0.1 points, favor strong-leg kickers in competent offenses with field-goal opportunity.

### Team exposure and handcuffs

- **Max players from one NFL team:** No hard cap. Allow 2–3 players from a strong offense when each is individually a value. Avoid 3+ players from weak or offense-uncertain teams, especially when outcomes depend on the same fragile QB/offensive environment.
- **Handcuffs:** Prefer high-leverage handcuffs to other managers’ RBs over mediocre backups to your own RBs. Handcuff your own RB1 only if the backup is a clear role successor and falls into the appropriate late-round tier.

## Risk tolerance

- **Risk profile:** Medium-high with role discipline. Chase elite volume and contingent league-winning outcomes after core starters are set, but do not draft several early players carrying active injury/availability uncertainty.
- **Injury history tolerance:** Medium. Accept a discounted elite player with a confirmed Week 1 role, but require a meaningful discount for active soft-tissue, knee, foot, ankle, suspension, or role uncertainty.
- **Age:** Be cautious at price with RBs age 29+ and WRs age 31+, but do not auto-exclude a proven player with strong routes and target volume if price compensates.
- **Rookies:** Favor rookie RBs with an early workload and receiving path, and rookie WRs with immediate routes/target potential. Avoid rookie TEs unless the price is endgame and the role is unusually clear.
- **Floor vs. ceiling:** Early rounds: target reliability through elite volume and stable roles. Middle and late rounds: favor ceiling, route expansion, and contingent workload over low-ceiling veteran bench depth.
- **Half-PPR tiebreaker:** When players have similar workload projections, use routes and targets as a tiebreaker. But do not ignore stable rushing, goal-line, yardage, and TD roles.

## Stacking and correlation

- **QB + WR/TE stacks:** Seek natural stacks only when the individual player is already the best value in the tier. A stack is a tiebreaker, not a reason to reach.
- **Potential stacks, subject to current platform team assignments:** Burrow + Chase/Higgins; Lamar + Flowers; Herbert + McConkey/Johnston/Harris; Purdy + Nacua/Adams/Stribling; Mayfield + Godwin/Irving; Lawrence + Brian Thomas/Parker Washington; Shough + a Saints pass catcher.
- **Same-bye stacking:** Avoid drafting 3 or more essential starters with the same bye when alternatives are equal. Two is acceptable. Do not sacrifice player value merely to avoid a two-player bye overlap.
- **Backfield correlation:** Do not roster a starter and handcuff by default. Do so only if the backup would inherit a valuable half-PPR-friendly role and the roster has enough weekly-starter depth.

- **Upgrade in close tiers:** Bell-cow/high-touch RBs; RBs with stable goal-line work; efficient downfield WRs with dependable routes and red-zone access; high-volume passers because of the 1 point per 20 passing-yard scoring rate.

## Roster construction checkpoints

- **After Round 3:** Prefer at least 2 WR and 1 RB. The acceptable alternative is 2 elite RB plus 1 WR if WR values clearly remain stronger in the next tier. No early QB.
- **After Round 6:** Prefer 2–3 RB, 3–4 WR, and either one value TE or a clear plan to wait. Do not have both an expensive QB and TE unless both fell substantially below cost.
- **After Round 9:** Prefer 3–4 RB, 4–5 WR, one QB, and one TE. If QB or TE is delayed, make a deliberate paired late-round plan.
- **Final roster:** Maintain enough reliable routes/touches for weekly lineup stability, then use several bench spots for contingent RBs and route-based rookie WRs. Do not fill the bench with replacement-level veterans.

## Final ten rules

1. Draft best player within tiers through Round 6; value volume and role before narrative.
2. In close RB/WR calls, weight touches/targets, goal-line work, then receiving role.
3. Exit Round 6 with two credible RBs and at least three WRs whenever possible.
4. Build to four or five WRs by Round 9–10, but not at the expense of a usable RB2/RB3.
5. Do not force a second QB for the QB-flex spot.
6. Buy elite TE only at a fall; otherwise wait or pair late TE upside profiles.
7. Use Rounds 11–13 to acquire contingent RBs and immediate-route rookie WRs.
8. Draft injury-risk elite players only at real discounts and after confirmed role/availability review.
9. Use stacking only to break ties, never to justify a reach.
10. Draft K and DST in the final two selections.
